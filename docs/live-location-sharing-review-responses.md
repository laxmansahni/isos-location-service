# Live Location Sharing – Responses to Architecture Review Questions

Responses to the Architecture team's questions on the [backend architecture proposal](./live-location-sharing-backend-architecture.md). "Where" points to the changed section. ADR-001, ADR-010 and ADR-011 are **Accepted**; everything else is **Proposed** until its open questions are answered; items needing another team are listed under "Still needs".

What was and was not read:

- **Read:** story SUB-5675 (supplied by the product team; the requirement baseline) and the Location Confidence & Impact PRD ([Confluence](https://internationalsos.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/PR/pages/6423838765)). The Location Confidence page is the **consumer's** PRD; it never mentions live location sharing, so it is not the PRD for this feature.
- **Not read:** the live-location PRD itself, epic [SX-76](https://internationalsos.atlassian.net/browse/SX-76), stories SUB-5354 and SUB-5652 (SUB-5675 is "Non-App", so they are likely the app-side stories), and the Location Confidence HLD, LLD and ADR pages. Statements marked "PRD (via HLD v0.2)" come from Architecture's HLD v0.2 and still need verifying against SX-76.
- Code facts come from this repository and from the middleware `Web.config`.

| 5 | PRD excludes continuous or high-frequency GPS but the document says at least 150 m | Two issues. The base proposal's 500 m / 1 per min and its AEC high-frequency profile did contradict the PRD; both are removed. The PRD's 150 m / 2 min is a **minimum spacing between sent points**, not a sampling rate. On iOS a distance filter keeps GPS running, so iOS uses significant-change (coarser) and Android uses balanced power with 150 m / 2 min. The PRD should define "high-frequency" as a number. The shipped tracker today uses a 500 m GPS filter at high accuracy, which is heavier than this | ADR-003 | Product defines "high-frequency"; mobile feasibility spike; heartbeat (12 h or 15 min) |
| 6 | Is AEC the same as the special advisory? Scheduling and local hours unclear | Not the same: "special advisory" is an alert severity in the app; AEC is a separate automated check-in workflow (`aecenabled`). The PRD's candidate is "one hour after an AEC", so AEC is a proxy. One generic event-window mode with a `trigger_type` (AEC or special advisory) and a window length means no schema change when Product decides. The window enables sharing; it does not raise the rate. Schedule is set by the traveller inside client-enabled types, in local time | ADR-004 | Product defines the advisory window and trigger |
| 7 | Client disables the feature: how handled? What happens to data already collected when the user opts in/out? | One fence rule for every way sharing ends. User opt-out: same, plus the local buffer is deleted. Opt-in again: new consent and session; pre-fence data is never restored. I read "opts in" as opts out or re-opts in; both are covered | ADR-005 | **Legal**: delete vs keep until retention (PRD open question 1); lease length |
| 8 | Location-based alerting: how handled? | Unchanged and not decided by this service. Location Confidence decides the place, Impact assesses, alerting notifies where it is already on. Key risk found in code: LBA today is fed by the Vismo stream from the same tracker (`aecenabled \|\| locationBasedAlerts`), so stopping the Vismo post would starve LBA. Interim: one tracker, two purpose-bound sinks, and retire Vismo once Confidence and alerting consume the events. Confirmed 2026-11-21: the product owner retires Vismo over time, once the native service does what Vismo does (ADR-006 section 4) | ADR-006, HLD 7 | Product and Legal sign-off on the interim; owner for Confidence live-share intake |
| 9 | PRD says Tracker and Quantum are in scope but the document says Tracker is out | The contradiction is inside SUB-5675: it lists "Manager-facing view … in Quantum/Tracker" as in scope and "Visibility in Tracker" as out of scope ("confirm with team"); the PRD (via HLD v0.2) has one read API for both. The base proposal followed the out-of-scope wording. The backend now serves both; whether Tracker ships a screen is a separate delivery decision | HLD 1.2, requirements C6 | Product confirms Tracker UI scope |
| 10 | Location Confidence: how is it different from Location Confidence & Impact? | The page is the Location Confidence & Impact PRD, now read. Location Confidence produces a 0 to 100 % score of where people and assets are **now** (30 min freshness), keeps its own signal ledger of history, and serves queries and summaries under RBAC, ABAC and segmentation, with Kafka and an Aurora store as dependencies. The Location Service owns consent, raw live points, history and replay, manager visibility and audit and **does not decide place**. Overlap: both store live points for a while and both decide who may see them, so retention, erasure and visibility must match. Findings that change the design: its 30 min freshness against our 12 h heartbeat candidate, its PRD putting realtime location out of scope (live-share intake unplanned), and its 500,000-person scale | ADR-006 section 1 and 3, requirements 3.4, C11 to C13 | Confidence team: owner and date for live-share intake, the signal contract and validity rule. Read its HLD, LLD and ADR pages. Privacy: retention approval |
| 11 | Phone location permission revoked or turned off | Consent stays granted (separate from the OS permission). The device stops, reports state, the server marks the device session suspended, the map shows the last point with its age as "not sharing", and capture resumes automatically when the permission returns. "While Using" gives foreground-only sharing; the current app accepts both Always and While Using, so the new module must make the background requirement explicit. A killed app is detected by the missing heartbeat | ADR-005 section 3, LLD 2.1, 4 | Mobile confirms module events on both platforms |
| 12 | Multiple devices: which has priority? | No static priority. The newest allowed point (by capture time) among devices with an active session wins, replaced only by a newer point; ties prefer accuracy then later receipt; future timestamps are clamped. Logout ends one device; revoke, disable and client change end all. The device registry already exists | ADR-008 | Product: phone-preferred rule or device cap? |
| 13 | AEC depends on silent push; iOS limits and delays it | Fair point; the base proposal over-relied on it. Silent push is now an accelerator only. The device re-evaluates policy on foreground, token refresh, every OS location wake-up, heartbeat and the AEC notification. The server accepts any point captured inside the window whenever the device learned of it. The pilot measures trigger-to-first-point time; no in-minute guarantee is promised. A guaranteed start would need a visible notification | ADR-004 section 3 | Verify push behaviour on target devices; confirm the AEC notification is visible |
| 14 | How are the traveller's local hours calculated? | In the traveller's local time. Zone order: the device's IANA zone (sent with policy requests and with each batch, plus a UTC offset per point), then the profile home zone, then the client default. The device evaluates offline; the server re-evaluates at ingestion using capture time and the point's offset. Daylight saving and windows crossing midnight are handled by IANA rules | ADR-004 section 2, LLD 5.1 | Product confirms traveller-local, not client office hours |
| 15 | When a user changes client, how does cache invalidation happen? | Treated as revoke for the old client and fresh consent for the new one. Policy cache is keyed by user, client and policy version, and the token carries the client, so a stale entry cannot match. The Policy Service also evicts on a client-change event (assumption). The old latest-position entry is deleted and the map re-checks population membership at read time. The device clears its cached policy and buffer for the old client | ADR-005 section 4, LLD 5.2 | IAM: does a client-change event exist? |
| 16 | Where is data stored: user region or client region? | The client's data region, resolved through the existing client-to-region lookup (the app already resolves region from the client's Salesforce id and loads per-region endpoints). The user's region is kept on the consent record to drive consent and retention rules, not storage location. Events must not carry coordinates across regions | ADR-007 | Residency of Location Confidence and Impact; clients whose users fall under stricter law (Legal) |
| 17 | ADR with pros and cons: in-house vs Vismo vs Amazon vs others | Comparison across drivers for in-house, Vismo, Amazon Location Service (Trackers), location SaaS, open-source servers, and cloud primitives as the build substrate. Recommendation: in-house. Vendor capabilities are labelled assumptions to be verified | ADR-001 | Due diligence evidence per vendor; delivery estimate and owner |

## Corrections to the earlier draft made along the way

- Storage engine: the proposal first assumed PostgreSQL, then I wrongly dropped it after reading only the middleware `Web.config` (SQL Server). The project owner confirmed on 2026-11-19 that the location database is Aurora PostgreSQL, with a local PostgreSQL for development. ADR-010 now records that decision.
- Impact consuming the location events and alerting subscribing to them is replaced by Location Confidence as the consumer (ADR-006).
- The `202`/"stored" acknowledgement ambiguity is closed (LLD 4.1).
- Policy is evaluated at capture time for buffered points (LLD 5.1).
- The single client-level schedule table became user-level windows with a client toggle (LLD 2.1).

## Evidence added 2026-11-21: unified `deviceinfo` API

The app reads and writes AEC and LBA flags with `GET` and `POST unifiedappsapi/deviceinfo`. Its body shows: `userSettings.salesforceAccountID` (numeric; the likely tenant id, so our `client_id` becomes a number), `locationBasedAlerts`, `accountAECEnabled` and `aecEnabled`, and in `deviceInformation` a `deviceID`, `partyID` and `individualId`, with the push token alongside. Effects: the tenant identifier question (client id) has a likely answer, and the server can verify that AEC or LBA is on instead of trusting the app (ADR-006 open items). The device `id` we store should be the same `deviceID` the unified API uses, so the two can be joined. The sample contained a real push token and email; neither is recorded here.
